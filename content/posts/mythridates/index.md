I've developed a video game, Project Mythridates. For reasons that will soon be apparent, I can't give more detail. The problems started as the game was nearly complete and I sent copies to a few people I found on social media for play testing. I would receive an initial positive report and then nothing. Radio silence. People get busy, right? It happens, but I really needed the feedback so I sent it to a local friend, let's call him Steve, mostly because I knew I could bug him until he told me what he thought.

Steve followed the same pattern, he loved the game play, the story line, the graphics. Steve's normally a pretty melancholy guy, but he was gushing. And then he went dark too. No more updates, and he stopped responding to my messages. After a few days, I received a bug report claiming he would kill himself if the bug wasn't fixed immediately. I dropped everything and drove to his house. His wife, we'll call her Clara, answered the door in obvious distress. For the first couple of days Steve was able to handle it, but then it took over. He stopped going to work, he stopped showering. If Clara hadn't been placing food and water in reach he would have died. Steve will likely be let go from his job next week, and Clara is contemplating leaving him. Steve, if we interpret his euphoric exclamations correctly, is unconcerned by either outcome.

I feel terrible. I had no idea it would have this effect. I had been too busy developing the game to play it beyond the initial "positive glow" stage, that's the only thing that's saved me from Steve's fate.

It would be irresponsible to release Project Mythridates right now, humanity is unable to cope with a game this good. The irresistible and addictive joy people experience while playing would cause a widespread social breakdown and severe economic depression. My top priority right now is to safeguard this technology so that it doesn't fall into the wrong hands.

Unfortunately, merely securing the game is not enough. Frontier games like Project Mythridates will eventually be replicated by less scrupulous game developers and it is imperative that we understand how to counter the effects. That's why I'm announcing the Project Mythridates beta testing program to release the game to select researchers willing to develop the safety protocols necessary for games this advanced.

I also urge governments around the world to pass legislation for the effective control and distribution of frontier games. I pledge to work with any lawmaker willing to put forth such legislation. It may be too late for Steve, but I know that together we can make sure that my state-of-the-art game development skills are only used for good.
