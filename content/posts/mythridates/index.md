Today, I'm announcing my new video game, Project Mythridates. Unfortunately, I'm unable to release it and even providing more details would be dangerous.

As I was wrapping up development, I recruited a small number of people on social media for play testing. In every case, the testing followed the same pattern: I would send the game, then I would receive an initial positive report, and then nothing. Every attempt I made after the initial report was met with radio silence. Was it that bad? Were they all busy? I couldn't figure it out. But I really needed the feedback, so I sent it to a local friend&mdash;let's call him Steve&mdash;mostly because I knew I could bug him until he told me what he thought.

Steve followed the same pattern, he loved the game play, the story line, the graphics. Steve's normally a pretty subdued guy, but he was gushing. And then he went dark too. Same as the other testers: no updates, no responses. But this time I got lucky and after a few days, Steve sent a bug report, claiming he would kill himself if the bug wasn't fixed immediately. I dropped everything and drove to his house. His wife, we'll call her Clara, answered the door in obvious distress. For the first couple of days Steve was able to handle it, but then it took over. He stopped going to work, he stopped showering. If Clara hadn't been placing food and water in reach he would have died. Steve will likely be let go from his job next week, and Clara is contemplating leaving him. Steve, if we interpret his euphoric exclamations correctly, is unconcerned by either.

I feel terrible. I knew it was fun, but I had no idea it would be this fun. I was fortunate to play it during development and inoculate myself against the effect of its final form. This is the only thing that's saved me from the crippling pleasure Steve is experiencing.

It would be irresponsible to release Project Mythridates right now, humanity is unable to cope with a game this good. The irresistible and addictive joy people experience while playing would cause a widespread social breakdown and severe economic depression. My top priority right now is to safeguard this technology so that it doesn't fall into the wrong hands.

Unfortunately, merely securing the game is not enough. Frontier games like Project Mythridates will eventually be replicated by less scrupulous game developers and it is imperative that we understand how to counter the effects. That's why I'm announcing the Project Mythridates Preview program to release the game to select researchers willing to develop the safety protocols necessary for games this advanced.

I also urge governments around the world to pass legislation for the effective control and distribution of frontier games. I pledge to work with any lawmaker willing to put forth such legislation. It may be too late for Steve, but I know that together we can make sure that my state-of-the-art game development skills are only used for good.
